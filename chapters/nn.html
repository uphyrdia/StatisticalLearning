\section{Convolutional neural networks}

Let \((e_n)_{n\in\ZZ}\) be the standard basis of \(\RR^{\oplus\ZZ}\). In this section, an input is a tensor in \(\RR^{c_\text{in}}\otimes\RR^{\oplus\ZZ}\otimes \RR^{\oplus\ZZ}\), where \(c_\text{in}\in\NN\) is the \textit{input channel dimension}; an output will be a tensor in \(\RR^{c_\text{out}}\otimes\RR^{\oplus\ZZ}\otimes \RR^{\oplus\ZZ}\), where \(c_\text{out}\in\NN\) is the \textit{output channel dimension}. Assume further that the input is of the form \[ \sum_{\substack{0\leqslant i\leqslant w-1\\ 0\leqslant j \leqslant h-1}}v_{i,j}\otimes e_i\otimes e_j, \quad v_{i,j}\in\RR^{c_\text{in}} \] where \(w,h\in\NN\) specify the \textit{width} and \textit{height} of the input, respectively.

\subsection{Convolutional layers} \begin{def}\label{def-convolution}

For \(\delta\in\ZZ, s\in\NN\), let \[ T_{\delta,s}:\RR^{\oplus\ZZ}\rightarrow\RR^{\oplus\ZZ},\,\,e_{n}\mapsto \begin{cases} e_{(n-\delta)/s} & \text{if }s\mid (n-\delta)\\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

Let \(k,s\in\NN, p\in\NN\cup\{0\}\) with \(w+2p\geqslant k\) and \(h+2p\geqslant k\), and put \(w'=\lfloor (w+2p-k)/s\rfloor\), \(h'=\lfloor (h+2p-k)/s\rfloor\). A 2D \textit{convolutional layer} of \textit{size} \(k\), \textit{stride} \(s\), with \textit{padding} \(p\), is the map \[ x\longmapsto \Bigl(\sum_{i,j\in \{0,1,\dots,k-1\}}W_{i,j}\otimes T_{i-p,s}\otimes T_{j-p,s}\Bigr)(x) \,+\,b\otimes\sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant w'}e_i\otimes\sum_{0\leqslant j\leqslant h'}e_j \] where \((W_{i,j})\) is a family of learnable linear maps \(\RR^{c_\text{in}}\rightarrow \RR^{c_\text{out}}\) called the \textit{kernel} of the convolutional layer, and \(b\in\RR^{c_\text{out}}\) a learnable vector called the \textit{bias} of the convolutional layer. The output has width \(w'+1\) and height \(h'+1\).

\end{def} \begin{thm}\label{thm-conv-equivariance}

For \(a,b\in\ZZ\), write \(T_a\) for \(T_{a,1}\), the shift \(e_n\mapsto e_{n-a}\), and \(S_{a,b}=\mathrm{id}\otimes T_a\otimes T_b\) for the shift by \((a,b)\) of an input or an output. A linear map \(K\) from inputs to outputs commutes with all shifts, \[ K\circ S_{a,b}=S_{a,b}\circ K\qquad\forall a,b\in\ZZ, \] if and only if there are a finite set \(F\subset\ZZ^2\) and linear maps \(W_{i,j}:\RR^{c_\text{in}}\to\RR^{c_\text{out}}\), \((i,j)\in F\), such that \[ K=\sum_{(i,j)\in F}W_{i,j}\otimes T_i\otimes T_j. \]

In particular, the linear part \(\sum_{i,j=0}^{k-1}W_{i,j}\otimes T_i\otimes T_j\) of a convolutional layer with stride \(1\) and no padding () is shift-equivariant: shifting the input shifts the output by the same amount.

\end{thm} \begin{proof}

\textit{If.} \(T_iT_a=T_{i+a}=T_aT_i\), so each term \(W_{i,j}\otimes T_i\otimes T_j\) commutes with \(S_{a,b}\), and hence so does their sum.

\textit{Only if.} Since \(K(v\otimes e_0\otimes e_0)\) depends linearly on \(v\), there are linear maps \(A_{m_1,m_2}:\RR^{c_\text{in}}\to\RR^{c_\text{out}}\) with \[ K(v\otimes e_0\otimes e_0)=\sum_{m_1,m_2\in\ZZ}A_{m_1,m_2}(v)\otimes e_{m_1}\otimes e_{m_2} \qquad\forall v\in\RR^{c_\text{in}}, \] and only finitely many \(A_{m_1,m_2}\) are nonzero, because the images of a basis of \(\RR^{c_\text{in}}\) have finite support. Put \(W_{i,j}=A_{-i,-j}\), let \(F\) be the set of \((i,j)\) with \(W_{i,j}\neq0\), and let \(L=\sum_{(i,j)\in F}W_{i,j}\otimes T_i\otimes T_j\). Since \(T_ie_0=e_{-i}\), we get \(L(v\otimes e_0\otimes e_0)=K(v\otimes e_0\otimes e_0)\).

For any \(n_1,n_2\in\ZZ\), \(v\otimes e_{n_1}\otimes e_{n_2}=S_{-n_1,-n_2}(v\otimes e_0\otimes e_0)\). Both \(K\) and \(L\) commute with shifts (\(L\) by the first part), so \[ K(v\otimes e_{n_1}\otimes e_{n_2}) =S_{-n_1,-n_2}K(v\otimes e_0\otimes e_0) =S_{-n_1,-n_2}L(v\otimes e_0\otimes e_0) =L(v\otimes e_{n_1}\otimes e_{n_2}). \] These vectors span the domain, so \(K=L\).

\end{proof} \subsection{Why parameters are shared across pixels}\label{sec-shared-parameters}

For \(q=(q_1,q_2)\in\ZZ^2\) write \(e_q=e_{q_1}\otimes e_{q_2}\), and for a tensor \(y=\sum_{q\in\ZZ^2}y(q)\otimes e_q\) call \(y(q)\) the \textit{value} of \(y\) at the pixel \(q\). For \(u=(u_1,u_2)\in\ZZ^2\) write \(S_u=S_{u_1,u_2}\). Since \(T_ae_n=e_{n-a}\), \[ (S_uy)(q)=y(q+u)\qquad\forall q,u\in\ZZ^2. \] Shifts never commute with a layer that has a nonzero bias, since the bias is supported on the finite output window. The right notion is therefore equivariance away from the edges of the window.

\begin{def}\label{def-equivariant-on}

Let \(\Omega\subset\ZZ^2\). A map \(f\) from inputs to outputs is \textit{equivariant on} \(\Omega\) if, for all inputs \(x\) and all \(u\in\ZZ^2\), \[ f(S_ux)(q)=\bigl(S_uf(x)\bigr)(q)\qquad\text{whenever }q\in\Omega\text{ and }q+u\in\Omega. \] That is, shifting the input shifts the output, except at pixels shifted into or out of \(\Omega\). The same definition applies to maps from outputs to outputs.

\end{def} \begin{thm}\label{thm-shared-bias}

Let \(\Omega\subset\ZZ^2\), let \(K\) be a linear map from inputs to outputs commuting with all shifts, and let \(b_q\in\RR^{c_\text{out}}\) for \(q\in\Omega\). The map \[ f(x)=Kx+\sum_{q\in\Omega}b_q\otimes e_q \] is equivariant on \(\Omega\) if and only if \(b_q\) does not depend on \(q\).

In particular, a convolutional layer () with stride \(1\) and any padding is equivariant on its output window \(\Omega=\{0,\dots,w'\}\times\{0,\dots,h'\}\), because its linear part \(\sum_{i,j}W_{i,j}\otimes T_{i-p}\otimes T_{j-p}\) commutes with all shifts (). A bias that varied from pixel to pixel would break this.

\end{thm} \begin{proof}

Let \(q,q+u\in\Omega\). Since \(K\) commutes with \(S_u\), \[ \begin{aligned} f(S_ux)(q)&=(S_uKx)(q)+b_q=(Kx)(q+u)+b_q,\\ \bigl(S_uf(x)\bigr)(q)&=f(x)(q+u)=(Kx)(q+u)+b_{q+u}. \end{aligned} \] So \(f\) is equivariant on \(\Omega\) if and only if \(b_q=b_{q+u}\) whenever \(q,q+u\in\Omega\). Any two pixels \(q,q'\in\Omega\) are related by the shift \(u=q'-q\), so this says exactly that \(b_q\) is constant on \(\Omega\).

\end{proof}

At inference, batch normalisation acts on the value of an output \(y\) at each pixel by an affine map. Allowing separate parameters at each pixel \(q\in\Omega\), it is \[ g(y)=\sum_{q\in\Omega}\bigl(D_q\,y(q)+\beta'_q\bigr)\otimes e_q, \qquad D_q=\mathrm{diag}\Bigl(\frac{\gamma_\ell}{\sigma_{\ell,q}}\Bigr)_{\ell}, \quad \beta'_q=\Bigl(\beta_\ell-\frac{\gamma_\ell\,\mu_{\ell,q}}{\sigma_{\ell,q}}\Bigr)_{\ell}, \] where \(\ell\) runs over the channels, \(\mu_{\ell,q}\) and \(\sigma_{\ell,q}^2\) are the mean and variance used for channel \(\ell\) at pixel \(q\), and \(\gamma_\ell,\beta_\ell\) are the learnable scale and shift.

\begin{thm}\label{thm-batch-norm}

Let \(\Omega\subset\ZZ^2\), and for \(q\in\Omega\) let \(D_q:\RR^{c}\to\RR^{c}\) be linear and \(\beta'_q\in\RR^{c}\). Let \(g(y)=\sum_{q\in\Omega}\bigl(D_q\,y(q)+\beta'_q\bigr)\otimes e_q\).

  1. \(g\) is equivariant on \(\Omega\) if and only if \(D_q\) and \(\beta'_q\) do not depend on \(q\).
  2. In that case, \(g\circ f\) is equivariant on \(\Omega\) for every map \(f\) that is equivariant on \(\Omega\).

In particular, take batch normalisation with \(\gamma_\ell\neq0\). Then \(g\circ f\) is equivariant on \(\Omega\) for every \(f\) equivariant on \(\Omega\), such as a convolutional layer of stride \(1\) with output window \(\Omega\), if and only if \(\mu_{\ell,q}\) and \(\sigma_{\ell,q}\) do not depend on \(q\). That is, there is one mean and one variance per channel, shared by all pixels.

\end{thm} \begin{proof}

\textit{Part 1.} Let \(q,q+u\in\Omega\). Then \[ \begin{aligned} g(S_uy)(q)&=D_q\,(S_uy)(q)+\beta'_q=D_q\,y(q+u)+\beta'_q,\\ \bigl(S_ug(y)\bigr)(q)&=g(y)(q+u)=D_{q+u}\,y(q+u)+\beta'_{q+u}. \end{aligned} \] If \(D_q\) and \(\beta'_q\) are constant, the two sides agree. Conversely, taking \(y=0\) gives \(\beta'_q=\beta'_{q+u}\). Taking \(y=v\otimes e_{q+u}\) then gives \(D_qv=D_{q+u}v\) for all \(v\in\RR^c\). As in the proof of , any two pixels of \(\Omega\) are related by a shift, so \(D_q\) and \(\beta'_q\) are constant on \(\Omega\).

\textit{Part 2.} Write \(D\) and \(\beta'\) for the constant values. Let \(q,q+u\in\Omega\). Since \(f\) is equivariant on \(\Omega\), \[ g\bigl(f(S_ux)\bigr)(q)=D\,f(S_ux)(q)+\beta'=D\,f(x)(q+u)+\beta'=g\bigl(f(x)\bigr)(q+u) =\bigl(S_u\,g(f(x))\bigr)(q). \]

\textit{In particular.} If \(\mu_{\ell,q}\) and \(\sigma_{\ell,q}\) are constant, then so are \(D_q\) and \(\beta'_q\), and part 2 applies. For the \textit{only if} direction, take \(f=\mathrm{id}\), which is equivariant on every \(\Omega\). Then \(g\circ f=g\) is equivariant on \(\Omega\), so by part 1, \(D_q\) and \(\beta'_q\) are constant. Since \(\gamma_\ell\neq0\), \(D_q\) constant means \(\sigma_{\ell,q}\) is constant, and then \(\beta'_q\) constant means \(\mu_{\ell,q}\) is constant.

\end{proof}

Estimating the statistics. The theorem says only that the mean and variance must be shared across pixels. It does not say how to estimate them. Pooling over the batch and all pixels is a good estimate when the data are roughly stationary, i.e. feature \(\ell\) has about the same distribution at every pixel. Then all \(B\cdot\lvert\Omega\rvert\) values of channel \(\ell\) in a batch of size \(B\) are samples of the same quantity.
