We begin with convergence of real sequences. Proofs are hidden until you open them.

Convergence

A sequence \((x_n)_{n\geq1}\) converges to \(x\in\RR\) if, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists \(N\in\NN\) such that

\[ n\geq N \implies |x_n-x|\lt\varepsilon. \]

Properties of convergent sequences

Boundedness

Every convergent real sequence is bounded.

Proof

Suppose \(x_n\to x\). By , choose \(N\geq1\) such that \(|x_n-x|\lt1\) whenever \(n\geq N\).

For those indices, the triangle inequality gives

\[ |x_n|\leq |x_n-x|+|x|\lt 1+|x|. \]

Thus the entire sequence is bounded by

\[ M=\max\bigl(\{|x_k|:1\leq k\leq N\}\cup\{1+|x|\}\bigr). \]

□

Uniqueness of the limit

A real sequence has at most one limit.

Proof

If \(x_n\to a\) and \(x_n\to b\), then

\[ 0\leq |a-b|\leq |a-x_n|+|x_n-b|\longrightarrow 0. \]

Therefore \(a=b\).

□
