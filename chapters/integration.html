\begin{def}\label{def-indicator}

For a subset \(A\subseteq E\), define \[ \mathbbm{1}_A(x)= \begin{cases} 1 & x\in A,\\ 0 & x\notin A. \end{cases} \]

\end{def} \begin{thm}\label{thm-indicator-integral}

Let \((E,\mathscr{E},\mu)\) be a measure space. For \(A\in\mathscr{E}\), \begin{equation}\label{eq:indicator} \int_E \mathbbm{1}_A(x)\odif\mu(x)=\mu(A). \end{equation}

\end{thm} \begin{proof}

The indicator is a nonnegative simple function, so the identity follows directly from the definition of its integral.

\end{proof}

The preceding result is . Its equation can also be cited as \(\eqref{eq:indicator}\).

Aligned mathematics

For \(A,B\in\mathscr{E}\) and \(a,b\geq0\), \begin{align*} \int_E (a\mathbbm{1}_A+b\mathbbm{1}_B)\odif\mu &=a\int_E\mathbbm{1}_A\odif\mu +b\int_E\mathbbm{1}_B\odif\mu\\ &=a\mu(A)+b\mu(B). \end{align*}

Here the usual convention \(0\cdot\infty=0\) applies.
